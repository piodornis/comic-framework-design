## Goal

Turn narrative and visual constraints into a reusable visual system rather than a loose collection of adjectives.

A strong style direction should help another designer answer the same visual question consistently without needing to rediscover the project's intent.

## Narrative-to-visual translation

Read relevant story-side material before setting art direction for a project.

Translate narrative facts into visual consequences without inventing new canon.

Examples:

- an old maintenance robot may imply wear, repair marks, replaceable panels, or conservative industrial forms
- a wealthy institution may imply controlled materials, repeatable signage, maintenance quality, or spatial hierarchy
- a dangerous environment may affect protective clothing, lighting, visibility, circulation, and surface wear

Treat the visual consequence as `INFERENCE` when logically supported but not confirmed. Treat genuinely new visual facts as `PROPOSAL`.

Do not assume that every story fact needs an obvious visual symbol.

## Reference interpretation

For each reference, identify why it matters.

Record one or more roles:

- style
- palette
- linework
- shape language
- material
- lighting
- composition
- character
- environment
- source-preservation

Extract specific traits such as:

- contour weight hierarchy
- edge hardness
- color saturation and separation
- shadow geometry
- level of simplification
- texture density
- silhouette construction
- focal hierarchy
- background detail density

Do not copy incidental content merely because it appears in the reference.

When references disagree, preserve the disagreement until resolved rather than averaging incompatible directions.

## Style-pack construction

Build a Style Pack from principles to rules.

Recommended order:

1. intent
2. three to seven visual principles
3. linework
4. color
5. shape language
6. rendering and shading
7. texture and materials
8. lighting
9. composition
10. character treatment
11. environment treatment
12. consistency anchors
13. variation allowance
14. avoidances
15. known failure modes

Prefer operational statements such as:

```text
Use one dominant outer contour weight and lighter interior structure lines.
```

over vague statements such as:

```text
Make it comic-like.
```

## Transformation profiles

Use Transformation Profiles only to define how source material is preserved or reinterpreted.

A photo-to-comic profile may preserve identity, pose, clothing, important objects, and composition. The Style Pack should then determine linework, color, shading, texture, and simplification.

When the same source must be tested in several styles, keep the Transformation Profile stable and vary only the Style Pack.

This makes comparisons meaningful.

## Exploration strategy

Generate the minimum number of alternatives needed to test a design question.

Useful exploration dimensions include:

- line weight
- saturation
- shape exaggeration
- rendering density
- lighting contrast
- material stylization
- background simplification

Change one major dimension at a time when the goal is comparison.

When testing an entire style direction, use a small representative subject set rather than producing a complete comic asset library.

Good test subjects may include:

- one face or bust
- one full figure
- one small environment
- one mechanical or material object

Keep all tests at `EXPLORATION` until the creator chooses otherwise.

## Consistency testing

Test whether the style survives changes in:

- character age or body type
- organic versus mechanical subjects
- indoor versus outdoor environments
- daylight versus low light
- close-up versus wide shot
- clean versus damaged materials
- quiet versus action-oriented composition

A style is not reusable if it only works for one reference image.

Define anchors that should remain stable across these changes.

## Conflict handling

Use `CONFLICT` when:

- a visual direction contradicts narrative canon
- two approved or locked designs disagree
- the requested style would erase a required identity or object cue
- a Transformation Profile requires preservation that a Style Pack would destroy
- project-specific rules contradict a reusable pack

Do not silently choose a winner between equally authoritative sources.

Offer the smallest set of resolution options needed for a creator decision.
