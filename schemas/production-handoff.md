# Design inputs for page production

Status: optional v1-compatible handoff guidance, 2026-09-17. Final asset schemas, import implementations and printer parameters remain Production/project concerns.

## Scope and authority

Design supplies visual identity, current reference selection, preservation constraints and approval scope. Story supplies exact text and narrative intent. Production applies those inputs to panels/pages, lettering and exports. A layout example is not automatically a new Style Pack, a character reference or a printer specification.

## Reusable handoff information

Identify the relevant design record, exact reference files/revisions, roles, active style/configuration, approved scope, continuity anchors and unresolved questions. Explain which details must survive cropping, placement or regeneration. Where relevant, include native pixel dimensions, intended crop, known color-profile information and provenance; leave unknowns explicit. Production determines effective image resolution at the final placement size against the actual delivery specification.

References and final panel instances have different roles. Do not label a reference sheet as a finished panel. Use links to the authoritative asset instead of copying it into Story and Design independently; document derived production versions and their sources.

## Artwork and editable lettering

For a workflow with separate lettering, plan images without baked-in dialogue, narrator boxes or balloons and preserve the storyboard's intended text space. Keep faces, essential hand actions, clues and continuity anchors out of those areas. A rough text-space overlay is a planning aid and should remain separately identifiable from delivered art.

Distinguish added lettering from in-world lettering such as a sign, interface label or symbol that is part of the depicted environment. Record whether such content must be exact and who supplies its wording. Do not silently remove story-relevant signs under a blanket no-text rule. If integrated SFX or painted lettering is intentionally required, record the exception, source text and revision limitations.

## Previous-comic intake

Preserve the original example and inspect a working copy. Record its stated role and the specific properties to reuse: e.g. bubble treatment, caption appearance or page rhythm. Do not infer approval of all visible art, a font license, current print settings or a new project-wide style from the example. Layout software/version, fonts, missing links and export presets are Production intake data; Design documents only the visual conclusions and their status.

## Review and correction

Check identity/reference fidelity, reserved text areas, safe crop assumptions and protected visual anchors for the requested use. Report problems with artifact/revision, location, expected/observed result and next owner. Typography/overflow and PDF preflight are Production checks; Design review does not certify print readiness.

A changed crop or balloon may hide a meaningful detail and require Story/Design review. A new rendering may require renewed identity/continuity checks. Keep routine authorized corrections within scope; do not promote EXPLORATION/SELECTED to APPROVED/LOCKED through file placement, an export or a completed task.
